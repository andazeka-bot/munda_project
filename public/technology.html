The anatomy of a textile light guide

The name says it all: textile materials are used as the light guide. It is not about lighting textile products — it is about the incredible flexibility that woven light guides bring to any product, textile or not.

Basic design

Woven light — fibre by fibre

Textile lighting systems consist of one or more LED light sources and a textile material — individual fibres or a fabric acting as the light guide.

Clear plastic optical fibres (POFs) made from PMMA are woven as weft threads together with warp threads of white polyester to form light-guiding fibre mats. The light-guiding fibres are combined in a ferrule that connects them to the LED modules.

  • Light emitted at the side of the fibre or fabric — lateral emission
  • Light output activated by individual light-scattering surface structures
  • Optional top fabric, diffusor fabric and reflector layer
  • Non-activated mats emit at the fibre end — axial emission for linear lighting
The light source

LED modules built for textile light

Integrated LED

Monochrome, white or RGB — the light source is fully integrated into the module.

Driver & thermal elements

Electronics and heat management live in the module — separate from the light guide’s installation space.

Mixer optic

A special optic in front of the LED distributes the light optimally to all polished fibre ends.

Why it wins

The advantages of textile lighting solutions

01

Optimal use of the installation space

The installation space of the light guide can be separated flexibly from that of the electronics. The light guide itself needs less than 5 mm for its entire design and folds into almost any shape. This opens up product upgrades in facelifts or relaunches with minimal adjustments to existing production processes — light can be integrated where other technologies can hardly manage it.

02

Initial investments and unit costs

No component-specific tool investment, unlike injection moulding — extremely cost-effective for small-scale production. Injection moulding only wins on direct unit costs in large-scale production, and even then its advantage shrinks as illuminated areas grow: tool costs rise exponentially with light guide size.

03

Short development times — fast prototyping

The light guide is a roll-to-roll textile product — virtually a carry-over part from a materials engineering perspective, re-assembled by adapting process parameters to the customer application. Tool-free technology and available electronic and LED modules enable extremely short development times, early visualisation that is almost ready for series production, and late-stage design changes.

04

Dynamic light

Assign individual fibres or fibre bundles to different LED modules and fascinating dynamic lighting scenarios become possible — ones that cannot be achieved with other technologies, or only with great effort and enormous cost.

05

Crash test — no problem

For the automotive industry, the decisive advantage is flexibility: textile lighting systems can be integrated even in crash-sensitive zones, illuminating larger surfaces in the vehicle than would be permissible with other technologies.
